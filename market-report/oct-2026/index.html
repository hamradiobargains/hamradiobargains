What changed since last edition

Edition 1 (July–September 2026) tracked 272 listings at an average discount of 36.6% and an average price of $409, with Yaesu the most-tracked brand (55 listings) and transceivers at 59.6% of the total. This edition tracked 57 listings at an average discount of 32.5% and an average price of $596, with Icom the most-tracked brand (13 listings, 22.8% of the total) and transceivers of all kinds at 61.4% of listings, against 59.6% in Edition 1.

Two plain differences sit behind those numbers. First, Edition 1 covered three months and this one covers just over one, so the listing count is not like-for-like. Second, this period's listings were pricier at the top end — a $3,200 flagship transceiver was the single deepest deal — and the average discount narrowed by about four points, sitting closer to the 25% line these listings are screened against. The deep deals were still there: 10 of the 57 listings came in at 40% or more below fair value.

About this report

This report is built entirely from real listings tracked by Ham Radio Bargains — a site that aggregates used amateur radio equipment listed for sale across the usual marketplaces and classifieds, and flags the ones priced meaningfully below fair market value.

Important context: this is not a snapshot of the whole used gear market. Ham Radio Bargains only publishes listings that come in around 25% or more under fair market value, with the occasional near miss just under that line — the shallowest listing this period sat 23.9% below. Every number in this report reflects that pre-filtered set, not typical used equipment pricing. In other words, this report shows what the best deals we found actually look like, not what a random used listing looks like.

Every number here comes from actual comparable sales, not estimates or industry guesses. This is the same underlying data and methodology used every day to flag listings on the site, summarized here so you can see the bigger picture: which brands and categories tend to produce the deepest qualifying deals, how often they show up, and what a genuinely exceptional find looks like.

Counting notes: a listing is counted once, on the date it was published, at the price and discount recorded that day. Two rows in the underlying records for this period were comparable-sales summaries with no listing attached; they are not counted. Listings that bundled extra gear are counted once, under the main item. Sold figures were checked against the site's sold record on November 5, 2026.

How we calculate "below market"

Every listing is checked against a set of comparable recent sales for that same model — not a single reference point, but a range. From that range, we calculate a median price, which becomes our estimate of fair market value. A listing's "percent below market" is simply the gap between its asking price and that median.

This is the exact same method available to you directly on the site — type any model into the "Is This a Good Price?" tool at hamradiobargains.com, and you'll see the same comps, price range, and median calculation used throughout this report.

How to use this report yourself

  • Don't rely on a single listing to judge fair value — a real price estimate needs several comparable sales, not just one.
  • A repeat model tells you more than a one-off: five Elecraft K3/100s qualifying in five weeks, all within a few points of each other, says more about that model's real street price than any single listing does.
  • A wide comp range (some listings selling for much more than others) usually means condition and included accessories matter a lot for that model — read listings carefully.
  • Seller vetting is a separate step from price. A good price on paper is still worth checking against seller feedback, account age, and return history before you buy.
