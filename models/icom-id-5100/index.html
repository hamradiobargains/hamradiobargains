About the Icom ID-5100

The ID-5100 is Icom's big-screen D-STAR mobile: a 144/430 MHz dual-band FM and DV (D-STAR) transceiver built around a separate controller with a 5.5-inch touchscreen (320 × 128, monochrome), introduced in 2014. It does 50 / 15 / 5 watts, receives 118–174 and 375–550 MHz, watches two bands at once (with one D-STAR decode at a time), and carries 1,000 regular memories plus a preloaded D-STAR repeater database that the DR mode uses to find nearby repeaters from the built-in GPS. The GPS does D-PRS position reporting over D-STAR — it is not FM APRS, and confusing the two is the most common disappointment with this radio. Bluetooth (the UT-133 board) and Android app control were options on the base package and standard in some Deluxe bundles, which is why two listings that look identical can honestly be different deals. The North American version is the ID-5100A; the European ID-5100E runs 144–146 / 430–440 transmit. It suits the ham who wants D-STAR done properly in a vehicle — repeater lists that find themselves, a screen you can actually read, and clean analog FM when digital is quiet. If your local digital life is Fusion or DMR, this radio does neither. Buying used, start with the head and its cable: the touchscreen controller is the radio's face and its most expensive part, the RF deck has no display of its own, and there is no speaker in the head, so where the body sits decides what you hear. Test the touchscreen across its whole surface, confirm the GPS locks, and ask the identity question outright — A or E, and what transmit ranges does it actually cover — because the answer changes both legality and value. Then ask whether the UT-133 Bluetooth board is fitted: two of our five recorded sales carried it, and it is real money. A complete A-model with Bluetooth at the $350 median is the fair buy this ledger describes.

Common questions

Does the ID-5100 do cross-band repeat? (a real forum question, asked about the 5100 on WorldwideDX and in QSO365's write-up)

It depends which version you hold — and this is the cleanest version test there is. The North American ID-5100A ships with cross-band repeater mode enabled. The European ID-5100E does not; owners enable it by removing a diode on the main board, a modification QSO365 documents step by step (at the owner's risk, warranty aside). So "does it cross-band?" asked of a seller is also "which version is it?" — get both answers at once.

What is the difference between the ID-5100A and the ID-5100E?

Region, and the band edges that come with it. Icom's service documentation lists the A (USA version) at 144–148 / 440–450 MHz transmit and the E (EUR version) at 144–146 / 430–440 MHz, with the serial number's first digits encoding the version. Cross-band repeat enablement also differs (Q1). Our system keeps the no-suffix ID-5100, the A, and by extension the E as separate identities — never pool their prices.

Does the ID-5100 do APRS?

Not the FM APRS most hams mean. Its built-in GPS feeds D-PRS — position reporting carried over D-STAR — and that is genuinely useful inside the D-STAR system. It does not beacon or decode on the 144.39 MHz FM APRS network. Buyers who wanted FM APRS are the ones who sell these radios disappointed; buyers who wanted D-STAR with self-locating repeater lists are the ones who keep them.
