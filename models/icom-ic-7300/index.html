About the Icom IC-7300

The Icom IC-7300 is the radio that dragged HF into the SDR era at a working ham's price — an HF plus 6-meter, 100-watt transceiver built around an RF direct-sampling receiver, with a real-time spectrum scope and waterfall on a 4.3-inch color touchscreen, in a box about the size of a loaf of bread. When it landed in 2016 it was the first time that receiver architecture and that screen showed up in an entry-level rig, and it reset what buyers expect at this price: SSB, CW, RTTY, AM and FM, a built-in automatic antenna tuner, an SD card slot, and a single USB cable that carries audio and rig control for digital modes. For a first HF radio, a POTA/SOTA pack, or a second rig that earns its keep, it is still the default recommendation — which is exactly why the used pool is so deep and turns over so fast. Buying used, the homework is specific. This is a direct-sampling front end, so in strong-signal company it can overload — check that the OVF indicator behaves and that the seller hasn't been running the RF gain wide open into big antennas; proper gain staging is the whole game on this receiver. Key it at full power on every band into a dummy load: the finals are generally sturdy, but you want to see an honest 100 watts (25 on AM) everywhere, and if you run an amplifier, ask about ALC overshoot — the 7300 is known for brief power spikes on transmit attack that some amps dislike. Work every corner of the touchscreen for dead spots, run the internal tuner through full cycles on several bands, confirm the cooling fan comes on under transmit load, and check the USB port connects cleanly — on a radio this digital, the port is half the value. Plenty of used units carry a MARS mod (under our standing ruling a MARS-named mod is a legitimate feature, not a defect) — just know whether yours has it before you price it. A clean, full-power 7300 with a working screen and tuner is the modern used-HF benchmark: deep supply, known behavior, and a fair value you can actually verify.

Common questions

What is the difference between the IC-7300 and the IC-7300MK2?

They are different radios and the used market prices them apart — our ledger carries the MK2 separately at a $1,200 median across 5 sales, versus $787.50 across 54 for the original. Listings that just say "Icom 7300" without distinguishing are the recurring trap; if a seller can't confirm which one it is, price it as the original until proven otherwise.

Does the IC-7300 have a built-in antenna tuner?

Yes — a built-in automatic tuner is standard, along with support for Icom's external tuners through the rear socket. (Some buying guides online get this wrong.) Test it across several bands before paying: a tuner that cycles fully and settles is part of what a clean unit's price rests on.

Is the IC-7300 good for digital modes like FT8?

It is one of the easiest radios ever made for them — one USB cable carries transmit/receive audio and CAT control, no interface box needed. Before buying, confirm the USB port enumerates on a computer and passes audio both ways; a dead USB port turns the hobby's favorite digital rig into a much harder sell later.
