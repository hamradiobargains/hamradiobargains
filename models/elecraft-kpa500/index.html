About the Elecraft KPA500

The Elecraft KPA500 is a compact solid-state linear amplifier covering 160 through 6 meters at 500 watts PEP on SSB, CW and data, built around a pair of VRF2933 FETs with an internal linear power supply — no separate supply box, and at 26 lb in a cabinet styled to sit beside a K3, it's about as unobtrusive as 500 watts gets. It needs only 30–40 watts of drive (never more than 40), switches bands instantly by sensing the RF — with any radio, using just a keying line and RF — and does its transmit/receive switching with silent pin diodes, so full break-in QSK has no relays clacking. It runs happily on a normal 120 V shack circuit (about 1,000 VA) and converts to 240 V without opening the cabinet: the fuse block is the changeover — fit the right fuses, flip the block, reinsert. Protection is forgiving and literate: a soft fault trims power about 2.5 dB and recovers on its own, a hard fault drops it to Standby, and the LCD spells the fault out in words. Introduced in 2011, it's still a current Elecraft product, sold factory-built or as a no-soldering modular kit. One thing it is not: a tuner. There is none inside — the matching KAT500 external tuner is the intended partner. It suits the operator stepping up from a barefoot rig or a tube amp who wants instant-on, no-tune, touch-nothing operation — the eHam field tester's summary was that it feels like owning "a 500-watt transceiver." It is a particularly natural partner for a K3/K3S/K4 with the optional KPAK3AUX cable set, which gives the radio two per-band drive levels so you can't overdrive the amp by forgetting to turn power down. When buying used, ask about fault and service history before cosmetics. HI TEMP is a real hard fault on this amp (heatsink limit 90 °C): sustained high-duty-cycle work — RTTY or FT8 contesting at full power — is what heats it, the fan's top speed is genuinely loud even if the lower speeds are near-inaudible, and it needs a clear 2 inches behind and above. One of our own logged sales carried exactly that fault, disclosed, and priced accordingly. Ask whether the finals have ever been replaced (two documented cases of VRF2933s failing despite the fast protection), whether it has ever snapped/crackled thermally (a known heatsink-bracket expansion noise on some units — an annoyance, not a failure), and whether there's any transformer hum, which on a kit-built unit usually means loose hardware. Confirm which mains voltage the fuse block is set for and which interface cables are included — and test it into a proper 50-ohm dummy load, since this amp watches dissipation, not just SWR.

Common questions

"What do I need to operate KPA500 with a K3 or K3S?"

Per Elecraft: order the KPAK3AUX cable kit. It enables Enhanced Mode — the K3/K3S holds two per-band power settings, one for KPA500 Operate and one for Standby, so you don't have to remember to turn the drive down when you switch the amp into Operate.

"I have 120 volts in the shack right now but plan to add 220 volts very soon. How hard is it to change the KPA500 to another input voltage?"

Easy, and you don't open the cabinet. The fuse block doubles as the voltage changeover: remove it, change the fuses to the proper amperage for the new voltage, turn the block over, and reinsert it. The amp accepts 100–125 VAC or 200–250 VAC, 50/60 Hz.

"What is the minimum and maximum power needed to excite the KPA-500 to output 500 watts?"

Elecraft's spec is 30–40 watts of drive, and 40 watts is a hard ceiling — more is PWRIN HI fault territory, and the manual says to set drive per band rather than rely on ALC. In practice it varies by band, mains voltage and load: owners in that same thread reported full 500 watts out for as little as 18 W in on 20 meters, 20 W on 10, and 23 W on 6 — so expect roughly 20–35 W on most bands, and never exceed 40.
