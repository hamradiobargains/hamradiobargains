About the Radio Shack HTX-242

First, the trap in the name: the HTX-242, catalog 19-1126 (1995/1996, Tandy), is a 2-meter-only mobile — not a dual-bander, despite how the number sounds next to dual-band rigs. Its own manual calls it a Two-Meter Mobile Transceiver, and that is all it is: FM on 2 meters, 45 watts on high power and 10 watts on low, receive from 136–174 MHz, 41 memory channels (one calling plus 40 standard), all 38 standard CTCSS tones for encode and decode, DTMF paging, dual VFOs, and an Auto Memory Store that sweeps and stores active frequencies with their repeater offsets. Judged as what it is, it is a likeable radio. Owners on eHam who run one as a cheap base or mobile say the audio is clean, nobody on the repeater can tell it from a big-name rig, and at under $50 working it is an easy buy. It is a 1990s Radio Shack mobile doing a simple job with real power — a first mobile, a packet / spare base, or a dedicated local-repeater box — not a modern dual-band, cross-band, digital anything. Buying used, test three things. Power: output depends on supply voltage, so a seller's "low power out" may just be a soft 12 V supply — a good unit should make its rated power at a solid 13.8 V (one eHam reviewer measured about 9 amps draw at full power). The mic: it uses an RJ-45 mic connector, convenient and cheap to replace, but owners flag the classic jiggle-the-connector intermittent — wiggle it while listening. And the display errors: the manual defines tF Err (trying to transmit outside the allowed range), PLL Err (PLL malfunction — power-cycle first), and EEP Err (EEPROM checksum — try a reset). A radio showing PLL Err or EEP Err after a reset is a repair, not a bargain, whatever the asking price.

Common questions

"Is the HTX-242 a dual-band radio?"

No — this is the most common misunderstanding about the model. The HTX-242 is a 2-meter-only FM mobile (transmit 144–148 MHz, with a manual-described procedure to extend transmit for MARS/CAP; receive 136–174 MHz). If you need 70 cm, this is the wrong radio at any price. Radio Shack's dual-band handheld of the era was a different product line; the 242's "242" does not mean 2 m + 440.

"What do PLL Err and EEP Err on the display mean — should I walk away?"

Those are two of the manual's three built-in error codes (the third, tF Err, just means you tried to transmit outside the allowed range). PLL Err means the PLL section is malfunctioning — the manual says to turn the radio off and back on, and to seek service if it persists. EEP Err means the EEPROM checksum failed — the manual says to try resetting the radio, and to seek service if that does not clear it. So: an error that clears with a power-cycle or reset and stays cleared is a note; an error that returns is a parts / repair unit and should be priced as one.

"How much power does an HTX-242 really make, and what supply does it need?"

Rated 45 watts high / 10 watts low, and like most mobiles of its era the output follows the supply voltage — owners report you need a genuine 13.8 V to see full output, and about a 9-amp draw at full power (one eHam reviewer runs his on a 10-amp fuse with 14-AWG leads). A unit that "only makes 30 watts" on a weak bench supply may be fine; a unit that still will not make power at a solid 13.8 V into a dummy load has a real PA problem. (One reviewer also reports turning an internal adjustment up toward 60 watts and turning it back down — ask whether a used unit has been cranked up inside; long-term, that is not a setting you want to inherit blind.)
