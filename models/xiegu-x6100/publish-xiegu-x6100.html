About the Xiegu X6100

The Xiegu X6100 is a self-contained SDR QRP transceiver that started shipping in November 2021 — HF plus 6 meters in a rugged little box with a 4-inch colour spectrum-and-waterfall display, a built-in rechargeable battery, a built-in automatic antenna tuner, and a built-in microphone. It makes 10 watts on SSB/CW/FM from an external supply (13.8 V, and never above 15 V) and 5 watts on its internal battery, covers the ham bands from 160 through 6 meters, and receives from 0.5 MHz up. It was pitched from day one as the budget alternative to Icom's IC-705 — roughly half the price at launch — and digital modes are a one-cable affair over USB, with CAT that speaks an Icom-style command set. It suits POTA/SOTA and backpack operators who want to hook up an antenna and be on the air, and experimenters: because the radio is Linux-based, Oleg's (R1CBU) alternative firmware — which boots from a microSD card, so pulling the card returns you to factory firmware — is a headline attraction, adding features including on-radio FT8 in later versions. Owners praise the tuner ("superb," per QRPer) and how easy digital setup is. When buying used, three things decide what a unit is worth. First, heat: the case is the heatsink, there is no fan, and Xiegu's own extended manual warns that charger heat on top of PA heat — worst on constant-carrier modes like FT8 — can damage the radio; run it on FT8 and feel the case, and expect owners who ran lots of full-power digital to have aged it faster. Second, the battery: ask for honest runtime on the internal pack (it is internal, not field-swappable, and the charger always charges to 100%, so a shack-bound unit left plugged in ages faster). Third, the receiver's known weakness is overload — near strong AM broadcast signals, owners found it could become almost unusable on 40 meters at night without an external broadcast-interference filter, so a city buyer should test it under their own conditions. Also check the firmware version (early firmware shipped unfinished, and at least one update left radios misbehaving) and confirm that plugging in external power is recognised and allows the full 10 watts.

Common questions

"Should the charging function be switched on or off to increase the output power when the Xiegu X6100 is operated via an external power supply?"

Switch the charger OFF whenever the radio is in operation, even receive-only. With the battery under load the charge controller can't manage it properly, and charger heat stacked on PA heat — worst in FT8, CW and RTTY — can overheat a passively cooled radio; in direct sun the manual warns the radio may be damaged. Charge with the radio off (GEN → RADIO SETTING1 → CHARGER), then switch charging off again for operating.

"The battery does not get charged" — why won't my X6100 battery charge?

Check the simple thing first: the CHARGER option in RADIO SETTING1 must be turned ON for the internal battery to charge from external power. With it off there should be no charge-LED activity and no effect on the battery — which is correct behaviour for operating, and the usual reason a "dead charger" turns out to be a setting.

"How do I connect the X6100 to the XPA125B amplifier?"

With the L4001 cable (not included). Connected that way the X6100 controls the amplifier's band switching automatically, ALC is established between the two so the XPA125B holds about 100 watts out, and you should set the X6100's own output to 2.5 watts or less to protect the amplifier.
