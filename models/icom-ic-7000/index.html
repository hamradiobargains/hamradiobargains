About the Icom IC-7000

The IC-7000 is Icom's "shack in a suitcase" — the rig that took the IC-706MKIIG's legendary all-band mobile formula and gave it a color face. HF through 70cm, every mode that matters (SSB, CW, RTTY, AM, FM), 100 watts on HF and 6 meters, 50 on 2 meters, 35 on 70cm — plus a 2.5-inch color TFT display with a real spectrum scope, IF DSP with adjustable digital filters, twin passband tuning, and a dual manual notch filter. The front panel detaches so the body can hide under a seat while you run the head up front, and there's even a video-out jack to throw the display on a bigger screen. For the mid-2000s, this was genuinely ahead of its class — a mobile rig with base-station manners. Why hunters care: it's the original do-everything box. One radio covers your HF DX, your 2-meter SSB, your 70cm repeater work, your portable kit, your Field Day station. Nothing else in its price class did all of that, and the used market knows it — clean 7000s hold their value stubbornly — our recorded sales put fair value right around $719 — which is exactly why they so rarely trip the 25% wire. The ones that do go cheap usually have a story — read the listing twice. What to watch for buying used: this rig is famously fussy about voltage. It wants 13.8V, and if the supply sags toward 10.5V the radio will reset or shut down on you — check the fuse holders first, because resistance in the power cord is the classic culprit for the voltage drop that causes it. The display is the other age item: the TFT's backlight and the polarizer adhesive don't love heat and humidity after 15+ years, so look for dim, washed-out, or uneven screens and price accordingly. The stock hand mic is widely considered cheap — get an on-air audio report before you commit. And the remote head has to be properly seated or the radio simply won't start, so if a seller says "it won't power on," ask whether the head cable is clicked in before you assume the worst.

Common questions

Will the IC-7000 run happily on my car's 12V system?

It'll run, but it won't be happy at full song. The 7000 wants 13.8 volts — drop toward 10.5V and it resets or shuts down outright, and even at a nominal 12V you won't see the full rated power. Owners who run mobile swear by a battery booster to hold the voltage steady, and the first thing to check on any used unit is the fuse holders: corroded or loose fuse contacts drop enough voltage to cause exactly these symptoms.

What's the real difference between the IC-7000 and the IC-706MKIIG it replaced?

The color screen is the headline — a 2.5" TFT with spectrum scope where the 706 had a monochrome display — plus IF DSP with adjustable digital filters, twin passband tuning, a dual manual notch, and 35 watts on 70cm (up from 20). Same basic idea (HF-to-70cm mobile all-moder), but the receiver grew up: digital filtering instead of optional crystal filters, and a display you can actually read at a glance.

The screen looks dim or washed out. Deal-breaker?

Not necessarily, but price it in. The TFT's backlight and the polarizer adhesive are known weak points on 15-plus-year-old units, especially ones that lived in hot cars or humid shacks — dim, uneven, or yellowed displays are a documented age issue. Replacement parts are scarce, so a perfect screen commands a premium and a tired one should knock real money off the ask. If the display is completely dead, that's a repair-shop job, not a settings fix.
