About the Kenwood TS-590SG

The TS-590SG (announced October 2014) is Kenwood's updated version of the TS-590S, the 2010 radio that proved a mid-priced HF rig could embarrass much more expensive receivers. The SG looks almost identical to the S — reviewers say the improvements are all under the skin — and it replaced the S in production; Kenwood states the S cannot be upgraded to an SG. What you get either way is the core package: all-mode HF plus 6 meters, 100 watts (25 watts AM), 500 Hz / 2.7 kHz roofing filters, 32-bit floating-point IF DSP, a built-in automatic antenna tuner (160–6 m), USB plus serial PC control with Kenwood's free ARCP-590G software, and a drive / receive-antenna output you can switch to feed a second receiver or SDR. The SG's headline fixes matter to used buyers because they are the S's known complaints, fixed in hardware. The original design showed a slight power overshoot on the first CW character, which tripped some linear amplifiers driven near full output; Kenwood fixed it in S production from early 2014, and the SG redesigned the ALC circuitry to eliminate it — Sherwood's test of an SG noted no ALC overshoot driving a legal-limit amp. The SG also re-optimised the AGC so strong close-by signals inside the roofing filter no longer desense the receiver the way early reviews measured, and added a built-in Morse decoder. None of that makes an S a bad radio — it makes "is it an S or an SG, and if it's an S, has the ALC fix been done?" the first question in any 590 listing, and it is why the two models must never be priced as one. Buying used, confirm identity and options before you compare prices. The SG takes two value-deciding options that are NOT standard: the SO-3 TCXO (tightens stability from ±5 ppm to ±0.5 ppm) and the VGS-1 voice guide / storage unit. A ledger sale at the top of the range bundled an MC-60A desk mic and the SO-3 — that is a different deal from a bare radio with the hand mic. The radio needs a separate 13.8 V supply rated 20.5 A peak or more, which is normally not in the box. Then do the working checks: full 100 watts on HF and 6 m, the internal tuner completing on a real antenna, USB audio and control actually connecting (owners in the TS-590 group report USB audio codec drop-outs as a recurring question — test it, do not assume), and the cooling fans cycling under a sustained digital-mode run, another topic owners raise on the group.

Common questions

"What's the difference between the TS-590S and the TS-590SG — can an S be upgraded to a G?"

Kenwood's own release note answers the second half: no — "It is not possible to upgrade previous TS-590S models," and the PC software is different (ARCP-590G / ARHP-590G for the G; the older ARCP-590 does not work with G models), though some SG functions were offered to S owners as the free TS-590S Ver. 2.0 firmware update. The substantive differences are redesigned ALC (eliminating the S's first-character power overshoot), re-optimised AGC, and added functions including the Morse decoder and the switchable drive / receive-antenna output. The radios look near-identical, so verify the model from the label and serial, not the photos.

"Does the TS-590SG have the ALC overshoot problem that trips amplifiers?"

That problem belongs to the earlier TS-590S design. The RadCom review of the SG states the cause and the fix plainly: the original showed slight overshoot on the first CW character, some users with amplifiers driven near full output experienced over-power tripping, Kenwood produced a fix implemented in S production from the beginning of 2014, and "in the SG version, the ALC circuitry has been redesigned to eliminate this problem." Sherwood Engineering's SG test likewise observed no ALC overshoot. If you are actually shopping an S, the question to ask the seller is whether that specific unit has had the fix — do not transfer the SG's clean answer to an early S.

"The USB audio codec keeps dropping out — is that a known TS-590 problem?"

It is a question owners bring to the TS-590 group in exactly that form ("The USB audio Codec keeps dropping out then comes back. I'm using Windows 10 and N1MM/MMTTY. Any suggestions?"). The radio's single USB connection carries both control and audio, which is a genuine convenience — reviewers praise running digital modes with no external interface — but it makes the USB path a working part of the radio's value. On a used unit, connect it to a PC, confirm the virtual COM port and the USB audio device both appear and stay up through a digital-mode session, and treat a flaky USB port as a real defect to price, not a cable shrug.
