About the Yaesu FTM-300D

The Yaesu FTM-300D — sold in the US as the FTM-300DR, the same radio — is Yaesu's mid-tier System Fusion mobile: 50 watts on 2 meters and 70cm, analog FM plus C4FM digital with Automatic Mode Select, and a colour display on a detachable head. Its party piece is real dual work: two completely independent receivers that can monitor FM and C4FM at the same time, including C4FM standby on both bands at once — something its bigger FTM-400 brother cannot do (you don't hear both C4FM signals simultaneously; the operating band takes priority). Its standby-watch feature is MCS (Multi-Channel Standby) over its Memory Auto Grouping, not PMG — PMG belongs to later Yaesu models (FTM-310/500 class) and no Yaesu, dealer, manual, or QST source lists it for this radio. Built-in GPS feeds a full APRS modem (1200/9600 bps), and built-in Bluetooth pairs with Yaesu's headset or a phone cable-free. It suits the mobile operator who wants Fusion and APRS without paying flagship money, and the base operator who wants one compact dual-bander that monitors the local Fusion repeater and the FM repeater simultaneously. Our recorded sales — 9 in-window sales, fair value around $311, range $220–$400 — so a genuine bargain sits under about $233, and condition and completeness matter more than usual. One ledger sale was explicitly MARS-modified, which is a feature for some buyers and a question for others — ask, don't guess. Buying used, start with the head. Separation is the #1 source of confusion on this model (see FAQ 1): confirm exactly what separation cable and bracket are in the box, because the answer differs by market and by seller, and a missing long cable is a real cost. Then test both receivers in both modes, confirm the GPS locks and APRS beacons, pair Bluetooth once (owners report non-Yaesu headsets pairing but not connecting — the Yaesu SSM-BT10/BT20 is the reliable path), and make a real voice test on simplex and through a repeater: a minority of owners report sudden loss of transmit audio (carrier and tones fine, no voice, mic or Bluetooth) with no forum fix — see FAQ 3. Run a longer full-power FM transmission too, and measure the output if you can: one owner measured ~40–44W and asked if Yaesu was shipping defective radios, another measured 50.1W on a service monitor — a single disputed report, so treat low output as a per-unit test, not a known defect. No programming cable is included new, and owners call Yaesu's ADMS software and firmware updating more difficult than they should be — a rig bundled with a programming cable is worth a small premium.

Common questions

Does the FTM-300DR come with a separation kit / cable?

It depends where the radio was sold — and this is the most-asked question about the model. US reporting (a QRZ thread confirmed by a US owner) is that the US-boxed radio included only the short head cable, not a long separation kit; at least one European owner reported a 3-metre cable and bracket in his box. So there is no universal answer: ask the seller to photograph exactly what cables and brackets are included, and price a missing long cable into the deal.

Can someone who actually owns an FTM-300DR confirm if mode is saved in a memory channel? I have the 400 and absolutely hate the AMS feature… I want to store those machines in memory as mode DN and not mess with AMS.

Yes — and this is a real difference from the FTM-400. The 400's manual states operating mode is *not* registered to memory channels; the FTM-300DR manual lists Communication Mode among the items stored per memory channel, and AMS TX MODE (AUTO / TX FM FIXED / TX DN FIXED) can be programmed per memory in the software. The asker confirmed it from the manuals, bought a 300, and reported it solved his problem.

Have suddenly lost my transmit audio. Receive audio fine. Can key up repeaters but no audio… Should I send it to Yaesu for repair?

Treat it as a radio fault until proven otherwise. In that thread, owners who tested 146.520 simplex still had a silent carrier — tones/DTMF got through, no voice on FM or digital, with hand mic or Bluetooth — and several reported the identical failure, with no forum fix found. For a used buy, that thread is the checklist: make a live voice test on simplex *and* through a repeater before paying, because "keys up fine" proves nothing about voice audio on this model.
