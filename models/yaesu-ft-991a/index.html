About the Yaesu FT-991A

The Yaesu FT-991A is the Swiss Army knife of the ham shack -- HF, 6 meters, 2 meters, and 70 centimeters, all modes plus Yaesu's C4FM Fusion digital voice, in one box that fits on a crowded desk. If you want a single radio that goes from HF DX to the local Fusion repeater, this has been the short list since 2017. Its forever rival is the Icom IC-7300, and the honest scorecard reads: the 991A wins on bands and digital voice, the 7300 wins on receiver feel and that gorgeous waterfall. The price of all that versatility is the menu system -- deep, non-alphabetical, occasionally weird, with a VFO A/B scheme that has launched a thousand forum threads. This is not a knobs-and-buttons radio; budget an evening with the manual. The used-market footnote that matters: early production runs earned a reputation for PA final failures, sometimes within months, so later serial numbers carry less risk. When you test one, run the internal tuner through full cycles on several bands (a documented grounding quirk makes some units tune intermittently), key up at full power on every band including a digital mode, and work the VFO encoder for jumping. A clean late-serial 991A is a lot of radio for the money -- and one box instead of three.

Common questions

What is the difference between the FT-991 and the FT-991A?

The 'A' added the real-time spectrum scope and multicolor waterfall display -- the original 991 shipped without them. If a seller cannot tell you which one it is, look at the screen: no waterfall, no 'A.' The A is the one to buy; the display alone is worth it, and it is the version the used market actually wants.

Are the early PA failure stories true?

Yes, they are real -- early-run 991s had a documented run of final transistor failures, and lab tests measured poor transmit IMD on some units. Later production tightened up, and plenty of early units have run for years without trouble. Ask for the serial number, ask whether the PA board was ever replaced, and test full-power transmit on all bands -- including a digital mode, where the failures often surfaced -- before you pay.

Do I need an external antenna tuner with it?

The built-in tuner handles about a 3:1 mismatch, which covers resonant antennas and minor sins, but not a random wire or a badly mismatched multiband -- for creative antenna situations, budget for an external wide-range tuner. Also note the documented tuner grounding quirk: intermittent tuning or odd SWR readings sometimes trace back to loose tuner-board screws, so run several full tune cycles during your test.
