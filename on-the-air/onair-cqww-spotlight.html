Weekend Spotlight

October 24–25, 2026 · CQ WW DX Contest, SSB

October 24–25: The Bands Will Be Full

The last full weekend of October is CQ World Wide weekend — the biggest phone weekend in ham radio. For 48 hours, tens of thousands of stations on every continent get on the air, 160 through 10 meters.

Here’s the thing: you don’t have to be “in the contest.” No score-keeping, no exchanges to learn, no log to submit. When that many stations are calling CQ, the bands turn into the easiest DX hunting of the year. Answer a CQ, trade signal reports, log the contact, move on. The pileups do the work for you.

What to expect, band by band

10 meters — manage expectations. We’re past the peak of Solar Cycle 25 (the peak was around late 2024), and the solar flux is running around 100 — down from over 300 at the height of the cycle. Daytime openings still happen, and a weekend this big pulls out every station with a 10-meter antenna, but it isn’t the wall-to-wall band it was two years ago. Check it around midday; don’t live there.

15 and 20 meters — the workhorses. Twenty is where most of the action will be, from daylight into the early evening. Fifteen opens when the sun cooperates — midday is your window.

40 meters — carries the evening. Comes alive in the late afternoon and stays open deep into the night. On a big weekend, 40 barely closes at all.

80 and 160 meters — after dark, the low bands wake up. Here’s the seasonal bonus: the nights are getting longer, and longer nights are exactly what the low bands want. From here through December, 80 and 160 get a little better every week — this is the start of the winter low-band season. If you’ve never tried 160, a weekend with this much activity is the gentlest possible introduction. Everybody’s listening.

The takeaway

October 24–25 is the single most target-rich weekend of the fall. Dust off whatever HF rig you have, match the band to the hour, and get on. Nobody’s asking you to compete — just be on the air while the world is.

Sources: CQ WW DX Contest, SSB — last full weekend of October each year (Oct 24–25, 2026); solar data from RSGB Propagation News, September 2026 (solar flux near 100, down from 300+ at the Cycle 25 peak); low-band seasonal improvement is the normal autumn pattern.
