About the Yaesu FC-757AT

The Yaesu FC-757AT is a fully automatic HF antenna tuner from the mid-1980s, built to partner the FT-757GX transceiver. It is a microprocessor-controlled box — unusual for its era — that handles antenna switching, impedance matching, and RF metering in one unit, with a maximum power handling of 150 watts. Connected to an FT-757GX (or an FT-980, via its optional cable), band changes and power control happen automatically over the supplied control cable; it remembers the settings that produced the lowest SWR on each band and recalls them almost instantly when you come back. It also carries a built-in 50-ohm dummy load, an automatic SWR meter (1:1–3:1 scale), and a dual-range RF wattmeter, and with the optional FAS-1-4R remote selector it can switch up to five antennas in total. Our recorded sales put fair value around $145 on 5 in-window sales, so a genuine bargain sits under about $109. What makes it worth hunting is that it completes a period Yaesu station properly — an FT-757GX without its matching tuner is only half the pairing — and the automatic-with-memory behaviour still feels modern. The honest catch is the matching range and the power ceiling: the manual rates the output impedance range at roughly 25–100 ohms on the low bands, so this is a tuner for reasonably behaved antennas, not a cure for a wildly mismatched wire, and 150 watts means no amplifier, ever. Buying used, the control cable is the deal. Ledger sales repeat the same completeness note — "with cable and original manual," "interconnect cables and manual" — because a tuner missing its FT-757GX control cable loses the automatic band-following that is its whole point (it can still be run manually on an independent 13.5V DC source with other rigs, per the manual, but price it as a manual tuner then). Second, test the automation itself, not just the match: one well-documented repair case had a unit whose WARNING lamp stayed lit, whose buttons did nothing, and whose Load capacitor rotated endlessly — traced to a dead memory-backup battery (an original 1985 cell reading 0.2V instead of 3V), a failed 5V regulator, and cracked motor couplers. A tuner that hunts, spins, or lights its WARNING lamp unprompted is a project, not a bargain. Confirm both antenna jacks select correctly and the motors stop at 1.5:1 or better, as the manual specifies.

Common questions

"Can I use the FC-757AT with a radio other than the FT-757GX?"

Yes, with limits. The manual provides manual power and bandswitching controls for use with any other HF transceiver when the tuner is powered from an independent DC source (13.5V DC, 300 mA max). What you lose is the automatic band-following and rig power control that the FT-757GX control cable provides (the FT-980 can also control it, via an optional cable). Price a cable-less unit as a manual tuner.

"Does the FC-757AT have a built-in dummy load and meters?"

Yes. Per the FT-757GX manual's description of the matching tuner, it includes a built-in 50-ohm dummy load, an automatic SWR calculating system and meter, and a dual-range RF wattmeter. Test the dummy load position and both meter ranges before buying — they are part of what you are paying for over a plain tuner.

"My FC-757AT's WARNING lamp stays on and the Load capacitor keeps rotating — what's wrong?"

That symptom set is documented in a detailed owner repair write-up (VK1ZDJ): the causes found, in the order checked, were a dead lithium memory-backup battery (the original 1985 cell measured about 0.2V against 3V), a failed 78L05 regulator feeding the microprocessor 7.5V instead of 5V, a failed comparator chip, and cracked vinyl motor couplers. Budget for board-level repair — a unit behaving this way is a project price, not a working-tuner price.
