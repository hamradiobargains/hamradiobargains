About the Kenwood TM-D710GA

The Kenwood TM-D710GA is a 144/440 MHz FM dual-band mobile launched in 2013 — the update of the 2007 TM-D710A that moved the GPS inside. It makes 50 watts (about 10 mid, 5 low) on both bands, receives wide (Band A from 118 MHz, including AM air band receive), and offers true dual receive — VHF+UHF at once, or same-band V+V and U+U — behind a detachable control panel with real buttons and a big switchable amber/green display. Transmit is FM only; there is no SSB. What makes this radio this radio is data: a built-in GPS in the control head, a built-in AX.25 TNC you can actually get at (1200/9600 packet with a PC), and a full APRS implementation developed with Bob Bruninga, WB4APR — station list, messaging, weather data, SmartBeaconing, a stand-alone digipeater, even a GPS logger good for 5,000 points. Add cross-band repeat, EchoLink Sysop mode and Sky Command, stir in 1,000 memories, and you get the mobile that reviewers kept calling the best APRS radio made — one was installed on the ISS in 2022. Kenwood has discontinued it (a successor, the TM-D750A, is only now appearing in FCC filings), so every example on the market is used. It suits APRS and packet operators first — Winlink and packet via the exposed TNC are exactly where it beats the Yaesu APRS mobiles, whose TNCs stay locked inside (see the FTM-400XDR draft above) — plus anyone who wants a dual-band mobile that can beacon, digipeat and hold a voice QSO as one self-contained box, no PC required. When buying used, completeness and two tests decide the price. The box should hold: MC-59 DTMF mic and hanger, DC cable, mounting bracket and screws, the modular panel cable, two line filters, both stands (vehicle panel-angle and table-top), panel holder and bracket, and the spare fuse. Programming and PC packet work need the optional PG-5G/PG-5H cable, which was never in the box — a missing panel cable or brackets is real money on a discontinued radio. Test one: receive sensitivity on both bands, wide and narrow, against a known signal — the D710/V71 family has a documented ceramic-IF-filter failure that makes a receiver go deaf over time (blamed on omitted DC-blocking capacitors), and whether Kenwood changed the filters for the 2013 GA is unresolved in the sources, so treat it as a test, not a verdict. Test two: GPS lock outdoors with the head where it will actually mount — the GPS lives in the head, doesn't receive indoors, has no external-antenna socket, and Kenwood's own manual warns to separate head from body if you hear interference around 438.8/443.8 MHz. And listen to the fan on a test transmission: family owners call Kenwood's fan logic dumb — it runs on any transmit, even a one-second 5-watt beacon, for about two minutes — so a worn, noisy fan is common negotiating ground.

Common questions

"I can not find this answer in the software help section or on-line. How do I program the Kenwood TM-D710 for receive only? (Weather frequency)"

On an unmodified radio you don't need to — it only transmits on the ham bands by design, so just enter the NOAA frequency. (The thread notes the exception: a radio modified for out-of-band transmit — the MARS/CAP mod — is a different case.) For one-touch weather, program a panel PF key (Menu 906/907) to "Wx. Ch."; the radio also has a dedicated Weather Alert / weather channel scan on US models.

"…the Kenwood 710G… Does APRS and packet radio right out of the box… does SSB and AM…" — "Are you sure about that?"

The sceptic was right to ask. The TM-D710GA receives AM — Band A only, it's the default mode on the 118 MHz air band — but transmit is FM only (modes F1D/F2D/F3E), and it does not do SSB at all, on either band. It's an FM mobile with an excellent receiver, not a multimode rig.

"I'm eyeing the Kenwood TM-D710G 144/440 MHz Amateur Mobile Transceiver APRS/TNC GPS/Echolink… Any thoughts good or bad about this radio? Are there any other radios that I should take a look at in the $600 price range?"

The thread's verdict: it's "a very nice radio and a true Dual Band radio," and the deciding argument was data flexibility — the Yaesu alternatives "seem to be able to do only Voice or APRS and are not good at doing them both at the same time," while for hooking up a computer for Winlink or packet "the Kenwoods work much better." If stand-alone APRS plus real packet is your use case, that remains the honest summary of why this discontinued radio still commands its price.
