Who fixes your radio? Authorized service centers for ham gear

Sooner or later every rig needs a bench. The question is where the manufacturer actually wants it sent — and the honest answer surprises most hams: nearly every major brand is factory-only. There is no big network of authorized repair shops out there. One brand names a single authorized center for amateur gear, one routes everything through its distributor, and one major brand doesn't publish a named center at all.

This page lists only routes the manufacturers themselves publish — "authorized" here means the maker names them, in its own pages. Plenty of independent technicians do fine work, and your local Elmer may know a great one, but they aren't the factory-authorized route, so they aren't listed here. If you're weighing a used rig — especially one that needs work — the repair route is part of what you're buying.

Before you ship anything, anywhere: get the authorization or RMA first (two brands below will hand your package back, or charge you extra, without one), put the paperwork in the box, and confirm the address with the company. Service arrangements change — everything here was checked against the manufacturers' own websites in October 2026, but a two-minute confirmation beats a radio in the wrong warehouse.

Yaesu — factory only

Yaesu USA publishes no authorized-center list; service is one factory operation in California.

Factory service

Yaesu USA, 6125 Phyllis Drive, Cypress, CA 90630

Phone (714) 827-7600, Mon–Fri 8 AM–5 PM Pacific · Amateur tech support: amateurtech@yaesu.com

Older manuals and warranty cards print 10900 Walker Street, Cypress — that address has been superseded. Use Phyllis Drive.

Icom — factory, plus the one named authorized center

Icom is the exception: its own repair page names a factory center and one authorized third-party center that takes amateur radios.

Factory service

Icom America Repair Center, 12421 Willows Road NE, Kirkland, WA 98034

Phone (425) 454-8155 (press #5) · Amateur, aviation, land mobile, marine · The only location certified for UL/TSO repairs

Authorized center

SAR Technical Services, Inc., 1792 Nash Drive, St. Joseph, MI 49085

Phone (269) 429-2334 · Amateur radios (no D-STAR repeaters), aviation

Either way, Icom's repair form must go in the box, and warranty claims need a copy of your proof of purchase. Posted non-warranty terms: a $125 minimum (one hour of labor) plus parts, and a $62.50 fee if you decline the estimate.

Kenwood — no named center published

This one deserves a straight answer: Kenwood's own FAQ says you may take a product to "any of our KENWOOD authorized service centers" — but it doesn't name one for amateur gear, and for amateur radio products specifically it directs owners to contact your local KENWOOD dealer. That dealer is your starting point. One more of Kenwood's own warnings worth heeding: they do not repair products at the Long Beach, California corporate office, so don't ship a radio there unless Kenwood directed you to.

Elecraft — factory-direct, authorization first

US service is Elecraft itself — but never ship blind. Elecraft has more than one repair location, so you contact them first, get a Repair Authorization, and ship to the address they give you with it. Packages sent without authorization cost extra.

Factory direct

Elecraft, 125 Westridge Dr., Watsonville, CA 95076 (company address — your ship-to comes with the authorization)

support@elecraft.com · Phone 831-763-4211, weekdays Pacific

Out-of-warranty work carries a minimum charge (typically $250) plus parts and return shipping.

FlexRadio — factory only

All service runs through FlexRadio in Austin, Texas. Open a HelpDesk support ticket first; if the radio needs the bench, they issue an RMA, and the radio ships to the address on that RMA — exactly as the RMA specifies.

Factory service

FlexRadio, Austin, TX · Phone 512-535-4713

Warranty and out-of-warranty repair both handled in-house. Dealers sell FlexRadio gear; they don't service it.

Alinco — service through the distributor

Alinco has no US factory service of its own. Its exclusive North American distributor, REMTronix, offers full factory-authorized repair and warranty service. They publish no street address — contact them before shipping.

Distributor service

REMTronix Inc., Manteca, CA 95336

Phone (209) 800-1945 · remtronix.com

MFJ and family — factory-direct, RMA by email first

MFJ Enterprises — along with Ameritron, Hy-Gain, Cushcraft, Mirage, and Vectronics — ceased on-site production in Starkville, Mississippi in 2024, but continues to offer repair service on in-warranty and out-of-warranty units. The rule is simple and strict: request an RMA by email first — mfjoffice@mfjenterprises.com — because packages delivered without one are returned to sender.

Ten-Tec — could not verify

We say this plainly rather than guess: no functioning official Ten-Tec service channel could be verified. The Sevierville, Tennessee address printed in old manuals and old repair lists is not confirmed as operating — don't ship a radio there on the strength of an old listing. If you have a Ten-Tec that needs work, an independent technician with a Ten-Tec reputation in the community is, honestly, the current route.

Xiegu — no authorized US center

No Xiegu-published US service center exists. Warranty repairs are handled by the manufacturer, per Radioddity (a major US Xiegu seller) — so your route starts with the seller you bought from.

Compiled from the manufacturers' own websites and checked in October 2026. Companies move, phone trees change, and policies get revised — confirm before you ship. Ham Radio Bargains doesn't repair radios and receives nothing for listing anyone here; if this page saved one radio from being shipped to the wrong building, it did its job.
