For My Family: Selling Without Getting Taken

  • Use the values on page 1 as your yardstick. Prices drift over time; you can re-check any model free at hamradiobargains.com's Price Checker, which shows actual recorded sales.
  • Selling piece by piece brings the most money — close to the total on page 1 — but takes time and effort. That's what my helper and my club are for.
  • A single offer for everything at once will be lower — the buyer takes on the work and the risk, and a discount for that is fair. But measure it against the page-1 total. An offer at half the total or less deserves a second opinion before you say yes.
  • You don't need to know whether it works — and don't guess. Nobody expects you to judge radio equipment, and an honest "untested" is a normal thing to say. Fair warning, honestly given: untested gear sells for less, and part of that discount is legitimate — the buyer is taking the risk. Two things close most of the gap. My own notes on page 1 say what I knew about each piece. And my helper can power up the valuable pieces and check the basics — in this hobby, saying exactly what was checked ("powered up, received well, transmitted on 20 meters") is normal, honest selling, and it earns back real money compared to "untested." A rig that was on the air every night is a known quantity, not a mystery — that's worth money too.
  • Get offers in writing, and take a day to think. Honest buyers wait. Anyone who says "today only" has answered your question about them.
  • If someone claims a piece of gear was "theirs on loan," ask for paperwork and check with my helper before releasing anything. It's a known trick.
The tower rule: don't let the radios and easy gear all leave before there's a firm plan — ideally a signed commitment — for taking down the tower and antennas. Removal takes a licensed, insured crew and can cost real money; a leftover tower can complicate selling the house. Tower first, or tower promised in writing, before the shack empties.
  • Unsellable items are normal. Old magazines, homebuilt projects, and parts often have no market. Donating them to my club or a charity (some give tax receipts) is a respectable end for them — not a failure.
  • My callsign and license: my family or club can notify the FCC and ARRL of my passing, and a close relative may be able to request my callsign. The ARRL also prints Silent Key notices in QST if you'd like that.
