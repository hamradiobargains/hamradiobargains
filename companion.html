Technician and up General and up Extra only

60 meters: General and up, on four fixed channels (centers 5332, 5348, 5373 and 5405 kHz) plus a small shared segment at 5351.5–5366.5 kHz. Power there is tightly limited, so check the rules before you transmit.

6 m, 2 m and 70 cm: every license class, Technician included, has full privileges. On 6 meters, 50.0–50.1 MHz is CW only.

US privileges under FCC Part 97, checked against the ARRL allocations chart. The Advanced class, no longer issued, sits between General and Extra on 80, 40, 20 and 15 meters. CW is allowed anywhere you hold privileges. This chart is a guide; the rule book wins.

Straight talk: this log lives in this browser on this computer. Closing the browser never touches it, but clearing the browser's site data can erase it, and Safari drops stored data for sites you stop visiting. That is why this page asks the browser to protect the log (see the storage check above), why Chrome and Edge can keep a copy in a real file on your computer, and why the ADIF export still matters: export after a session, load it into LoTW or your regular logbook, and that file is yours and works everywhere.

US license details come from the FCC’s public amateur radio records, served by Callook. Records for Canada, Australia, Germany and the Czech Republic come from HamDB; country, zone and reference-point data for all callsigns come from HamQTH’s DXCC tables. Your log never leaves this browser unless you export it.
