About the Heathkit SA-2060A

The Heathkit SA-2060A Deluxe Antenna Tuner is a big manual roller-inductor tuner from Heathkit's kit era — designed to operate 160 through 10 meters, matching balanced or unbalanced feed lines, single-wire and ladder lines, at what the manual calls the full legal power limit of your station. It combines the SA-2040 tuner design with most of the HM-2140 dual wattmeter: two separate meters read forward power (200/2,000W ranges) and reflected power (50/500W ranges) at ±5% accuracy, with direct SWR readings of 1:1 to 3:1 on the reflected meter. The matching network is a continuously variable roller inductor with two variable capacitors — no band switching from 1.8 to 30 MHz, including the WARC and MARS frequencies — with silver-plated straps and roller contact to keep losses down, a front-panel turns counter (displayed number divided by ten gives active turns) so you can return to logged settings, and a built-in 4:1 balun for balanced lines. Our recorded sales put fair value around $250 on 9 in-window sales, so a genuine bargain sits under about $188. The Heathkit catch is the one that applies to every Heathkit: it was sold as a kit, so the builder is half the product. Two SA-2060As can be the same model and very different tuners — one soldered by a careful engineer, one by a first-timer in 1984. The ledger underlines the point at both extremes: an *unbuilt, never-assembled* kit changed hands at $600 (flagged in the ledger as not comparable to an assembled unit — it's a collector price, not a tuner price), while working assembled units cluster at $200–$365. Buying used, judge the build before the badge. Open it up or ask for inside photos: look for clean solder work, unburned roller-inductor contact surfaces, and silver straps intact; roll the inductor through its full travel and confirm the roller contact doesn't jump the winding (the manual itself warns against forcing it past its end stops) and the counter tracks smoothly. Test both meters on both ranges — one ledger sale notes "forward and SWR meters work but other meter functions need work," and meter faults are the recurring condition note on this model. Confirm the balun is present (some sales list it as included/optional — ask) and the original manual is a genuine plus for logging charts. A smooth-rolling, fully metering, cleanly built SA-2060A at $250 is fair; the same money for a rough build with a dead meter range is not.

Common questions

"Is the SA-2060A a kit or a factory-built tuner — and does it matter?"

It was sold as a Heathkit kit, so every unit was assembled by its first owner — and yes, it matters more than cosmetics. Assembly quality decides solder integrity, roller alignment, and meter calibration. The market prices the extremes honestly: a never-assembled unbuilt kit sold as a collector item ($600 in our ledger, excluded from the comparable set), while assembled working units trade around $200–$365. Ask who built it if the seller knows, and inspect the inside work before paying a top-of-range price.

"Will the SA-2060A tune ladder line / balanced feed?"

Yes — that's a design feature, not a workaround. The manual specifies matching balanced or unbalanced feed lines and single-wire and ladder lines, and a 4:1 (balanced-to-unbalanced) balun coil on the output side permits balanced lines. Some ledger sales describe the balun as "optional balun included," so confirm it's fitted on the specific unit you're buying.

"What do the SA-2060A's meters actually measure?"

Two separate meters (the design carries over most of the HM-2140 dual wattmeter): forward power in 200W and 2,000W ranges, reflected power in 50W and 500W ranges, ±5% accuracy, with SWR read directly from 1:1 to 3:1 on the reflected meter. Because the meters are half the product, test all ranges — a ledger sale with partial meter function priced accordingly ($240, "other meter functions need work").
