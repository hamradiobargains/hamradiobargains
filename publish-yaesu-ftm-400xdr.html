About the Yaesu FTM-400XDR

The Yaesu FTM-400XDR is a 144/430 MHz dual-band mobile — analog FM plus Yaesu's C4FM System Fusion digital, with Automatic Mode Select to switch between them — built around a detachable 3.5-inch colour touchscreen control head. It makes 50, 20 or 5 watts on both bands, carries 500 memories per band backed up to a microSD card, and receives wide, including the air band. The XDR is the refresh of the original 2013 FTM-400DR, and Yaesu's own distinction is the GPS: an enhanced module with better accuracy and faster time-to-first-fix. Built-in GPS plus built-in APRS (1200/9600) means the radio beacons, tracks and messages stand-alone, and owners run voice on the A band while APRS works the B band at the same time — the trick the smaller FTM-100 doesn't do well. Add the Fusion extras (Digital Group Monitor, Smart Navigation, WIRES-X portable-node use) and a thermostat-controlled fan, and it's easy to see why owners keep them: one RadioReference poster owns three. Yaesu has discontinued the model, citing component availability, and files it under Legacy. It suits mobile operators who want APRS and Fusion in one box with a big, readable screen, and shack users who like the touchscreen for messaging. If your priority is packet radio with an external TNC, know the limit up front: the FTM-400's APRS is self-contained — it will output received packets, but you cannot externally control a TNC the way you can on Kenwood's D710 (see the TM-D710GA draft below), and it doesn't stand-alone digipeat. When buying used, confirm it is actually an XDR and not an original DR — check the model plate, not the listing title, because the DR's slow GPS lock is exactly what the XDR fixed. Test GPS lock outdoors from a cold start where the head will live. Exercise every corner of the touchscreen: it's a resistive panel that needs a firm, centred press even when healthy, and nearly everything runs through it, so a dead panel is a value-killer. Make sure head, body, control cable, DTMF mic and DC lead all come together — the pair is the radio — and remember the microphone plugs into the main body, not the head, with no mic extension in the box, which matters for under-seat installs. Finally, run it at 50 watts and confirm the fan cycles on and off again as it cools.

Common questions

"FTM-400XDR 'B' band transmits APRS data during 'A' band receive" — my APRS beacons on B keep interrupting what I'm hearing on A. Has anyone figured this out?

Yes — it's a settings issue, not a fault. Set the data band assignment (Menu → Data → Data Band Select: B band TX, A band RX), then set Data Squelch to TX/RX BAND (Menu → Data → Data Squelch → APRS), per p.198 of the operating manual / p.16 of the APRS manual. The poster who asked confirmed this fixed it — with the caveat that unless a band is tuned to 144.390 MHz, APRS packets won't decode and display even though other stations see yours.

"I just recently installed my Yaesu FTM-400XDR… I cannot get my location to display on APRS.fi. I know I am transmitting out… Any help with what may be the issue is appreciated!"

aprs.fi doesn't hear your radio directly — it republishes positions fed into APRS-IS by iGates. If your position report isn't hitting an iGate, you won't appear on the map no matter how well the radio works. In this poster's case an iGate was about 7 nautical miles away and responders suspected a radio setting; the general lesson stands — first confirm a local iGate/digipeater path exists before blaming the rig.

"I am receiving and hearing the repeater but can't be heard. I have put in the squelch tone 136.5 and still get no response."

Setting the tone frequency isn't enough — the radio also has to be told to send it. Press F, find SQL, and cycle until the display shows T-TX (tone transmit), and confirm the repeater offset is right. Both posters confirmed T-TX was the fix. It's the single most common new-owner trap on this radio.
